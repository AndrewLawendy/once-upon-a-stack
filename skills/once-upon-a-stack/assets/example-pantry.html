Why does Hazel (the cache) keep a pantry?

The problem

Every morning, the hedgehogs (requests from your app) want acorns (pieces of data). All the real acorns live with Mort (the database), in a storehouse on the far side of the valley. He never loses one, but the walk is long and he can only help a few animals at a time.

The strange part is that nearly everyone wants the same few acorns.

For grown-ups

Mort (the database) and the long walk
A database read costs time and a connection. Reading from disk or across a network is much slower than reading from memory.
The same few acorns
Traffic is often uneven: a small share of the data gets most of the reads. That's what makes caching worth doing.

Why does Hazel (the cache) keep a pantry?

Wait, how does she know a copy is too old?

She gives every copy a throw-away time (the TTL). When that time is up, the copy goes, and the next hedgehog (a request) sends her back to Mort (the database) for a fresh one.

Nobody has to tell her the acorn changed. The clock does the work.

Why does Hazel (the cache) keep a pantry?

The price

The pantry holds copies, not the real acorns. If Mort (the database) swaps an acorn (a piece of data) for a better one, the copy held by Hazel (the cache) is out of date until its time runs out, and Pip (the app) gets the old one without knowing. That's the moment Mildew (stale data) waits for.

A shorter time means fewer old acorns, but more walks to Mort (the database). Someone has to choose. And when the small pantry fills up, Hazel (the cache) has to choose what to throw away.

For grown-ups

Mildew (stale data)
Stale data: a cached value that no longer matches the database.
Choosing what to throw out
Eviction, often removing the least recently used item (LRU).
