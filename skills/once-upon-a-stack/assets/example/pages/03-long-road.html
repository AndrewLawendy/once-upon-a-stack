A Slice, Now

Every slice takes the long road

The first slice is lovely. It's also slow: the order goes all the way to the farm, and the slice comes all the way back. Step through it.

Then the next person wants the same apple pie, and the whole trip happens again. At lunch, the [[Baker|the database]] spends all day baking the same pie over and over, and every order waits behind the others.

What people see: pages that load slowly, then slower at the busiest hour, and finally time out.

For grown-ups

Why a database trip is the slow path
Verified Facebook uses memcache "to reduce the frequency of fetching data along more expensive paths such as database queries", and "to lighten the read load on our databases".
The same pie, over and over
Verified Some keys are very hot: "a single key can account for 20% of a server's requests". Inferred A few popular things can dominate a server's work.
Why it gets worse at peak
Inferred A database has a fixed number of connections and disk and CPU budget. Past that, requests queue, so every request waits for the ones ahead of it.

Nishtala et al., NSDI 2013, s2, s3.2, s3.3
